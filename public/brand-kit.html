05 — Voice & tone

How WP Flame speaks

Direct, technical, confident — but never condescending. We talk to developers and site owners who care about performance but don't want to wade through jargon to understand their site.

Show, don't tell

Lead with the visual. A flame graph speaks louder than a paragraph about performance. Screenshots, data, and real examples over abstract claims.

Specific over vague

"WooCommerce added 95ms during init" beats "your plugins are slow." Always name names, always show the number.

Developer-friendly, not developer-exclusive

A site owner should understand the flame graph. A developer should find the data they need. Write for both without dumbing down.

Calm authority

No exclamation marks in marketing. No "supercharge" or "turbocharge." Performance is serious work — the tone should match.

Do

  • "See exactly where your request spends its time"
  • "Elementor loaded 847KB of JSON on every page"
  • "Zero dependencies. No SaaS. No PHP extensions."
  • "Your homepage: 847ms. Here's why."

Don't

  • "Supercharge your WordPress performance!"
  • "Blazing fast insights for your website!"
  • "The ultimate all-in-one performance solution"
  • "Unlock the power of performance monitoring"
06 — Usage rules

Brand guardrails

Consistency matters more than creativity. These rules keep WP Flame looking sharp across every touchpoint.

Logo clear space

Maintain clear space equal to the height of the icon mark on all sides. Never place the logo on busy backgrounds or over photographs.

Icon, not logo, at small sizes

Below 120px wide, use the icon mark alone. The wordmark becomes illegible at small sizes. The icon works down to 16px (favicon).

One serif phrase per section

Instrument Serif is an accent, not a workhorse. Use it for a single tagline or emphasis phrase. If everything is emphasised, nothing is.

Flame graph as hero visual

When possible, lead with an actual flame graph screenshot or mockup. The product is its own best marketing — show the interface, not stock imagery.

Dark-first design

Default to dark backgrounds (Carbon 800–900) for marketing, landing pages, and social. Light mode exists for documentation and the WordPress admin context.

Never rotate or distort the icon

The stacked bars must always be horizontal. Don't rotate, skew, add effects, or animate the icon mark beyond simple opacity/scale transitions.
